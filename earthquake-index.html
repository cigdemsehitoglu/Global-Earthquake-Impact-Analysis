Global Earthquake Impact Analysis:
Public Health Toll and Economic Losses from Major Seismic Events

A reproducible ranking of the most harmful earthquake event types by human casualties and economic damage

Created on: October 7, 2026

1. Synopsis

Earthquakes remain one of the most destructive natural hazards worldwide. This analysis examines a curated dataset of major seismic events to answer two practical questions:

  • Which types of earthquake-related outcomes cause the greatest harm to population health?
  • Which types of earthquake-related outcomes cause the greatest economic damage?

Results are presented as ranked bar charts so that decision-makers can quickly identify the event categories that deserve the highest priority for preparedness and mitigation investment.

2. Data Processing

The underlying data are derived from publicly available earthquake impact compilations (USGS, EM-DAT style records) covering the period 1990–2024. For reproducibility the analysis uses a cleaned, event-level summary with the following key fields:

3. Public Health Impacts

Total fatalities and injuries were aggregated by impact category. The chart below ranks categories by the combined health burden (deaths + injuries).

Building Collapse is by far the leading cause of both deaths and injuries, followed by Tsunami and Landslide / Avalanche. These three categories together account for the large majority of the public-health toll.

4. Economic Consequences

Economic losses (in million USD) were summed by the same impact categories and ranked.

While Building Collapse still ranks high, Tsunami and Infrastructure Failure rise in importance when the metric is pure economic damage. The ranking is therefore not identical to the health ranking.

5. Health vs Economic Ranking Comparison

The dual-axis view highlights categories that are dangerous for people but less costly in pure dollar terms, and vice-versa.

Key takeaway: Preparedness resources should not be allocated solely by economic loss. Categories such as Landslide cause substantial loss of life relative to their recorded dollar damage.

7. Conclusions

  • Across the examined period, Building Collapse dominates the public-health impact ranking.
  • Tsunami and large-scale Infrastructure Failure dominate the economic-loss ranking.
  • The two rankings are related but not identical; policy that optimizes only one dimension will miss important risks.
  • Reproducible, open analyses of this type allow planners to update priorities as new events are recorded.
